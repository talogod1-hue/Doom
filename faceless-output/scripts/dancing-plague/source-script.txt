In July 1518, a woman named Frau Troffea stepped into a street in Strasbourg and began to dance. She didn't stop for days. Within a week, dozens had joined her. By late summer, hundreds of people were dancing through the city, many until they collapsed. Some died of heart attacks, strokes, or sheer exhaustion. Town officials, who had no answer, hired musicians and built a dance hall, thinking the crowd could dance the curse out of its system. It didn't work. Historians still argue about the cause. Ergot poisoning, mass hysteria, and stress are all on the table. What we know is that a city lost control of its own bodies, and nobody could explain why.
